# Problem selection

Use when the requested outcome is uncertain or the user asks what engineering work matters next. A clear implementation request does not need another discovery phase. Separate an explicit requirement from a proposed means: question consequential assumptions without silently replacing the user's chosen scope.

## Build an evidence-backed problem statement

Look at the affected workflow and available issue, incident, support, or code evidence. Separate the requested feature from the blocked outcome. Preserve unresolved signals in an existing task record when useful; repeated independent observations carry more weight than copies of one complaint. Treat a proposed common cause as a hypothesis and check cases it would not explain. Similar symptoms can require separate solutions. Small, well-understood improvements can proceed immediately; uncertain opportunities can be parked, tested, split, or dropped.

For each serious candidate, identify the affected actor, observable friction, conditions, consequence, source/date, and what is still inferred. Absence of telemetry is unknown impact, not zero impact. Distinguish today's constraints from historical incidents and independent evidence from repeated reporting of the same event. Use only sources relevant to the task; missing stakeholder access does not justify inventing interviews, adoption, or agreement.

## Choose what deserves investment

Compare plausible candidates against the user's objective: consequence and reach, urgency or cost of delay, evidence confidence, implementation and ongoing ownership cost, and the work displaced. Use ranges or qualitative judgments when quantities are unavailable; a weighted score cannot manufacture certainty. A rare severe failure may outweigh frequent annoyance. An active incident needs containment before exploratory prioritization.

Consider whether configuration, documentation, an existing capability, a local repair, or a responsibility change would meet the outcome before proposing a platform. A broad solution earns its scope by addressing demonstrated needs without imposing incompatible policy on its consumers. Identify who could own operation and adoption; mark unconfirmed ownership as a dependency, not a commitment made on another team's behalf.

Choose the smallest next action that changes the decision: inspect an affected workflow, measure a specific gap, test a risky assumption, or implement an already supported fix. State what result would make the proposal lose to its strongest alternative. When parking an opportunity, identify an observable revisit trigger through the existing planning mechanism; do not promise background monitoring.

Finish with the recommended problem, why it outranks the alternatives, confidence limits, and the next authorized action. Carry its outcome and unresolved assumption into [execution strategy](execution-strategy.md) only when delivery planning needs it. A recommendation is a valid endpoint for discovery-only work.

Source inspiration: [Lalit Maganti, How I Find Problems to Solve as a Staff Engineer](https://lalitm.com/post/find-problems-staff-engineer/), read 2026-09-20. The article's experience is primarily infrastructure and developer tools with bottom-up autonomy. Prioritization, evidence handling, and execution boundaries here are this collection's adaptation, not a universal organizational model.
